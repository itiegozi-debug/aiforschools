The learning hub

Understand AI. Think for yourself.

Start with the basics, then explore the questions that matter in school. These short guides are designed to help students become informed, careful users of AI.

01 · THE BASICS

What is artificial intelligence?

AI is technology that can perform tasks that usually require human-like abilities, such as recognizing patterns, generating text, or identifying objects.

Remember: AI doesn't think or understand exactly like a person. It generates outputs based on patterns in data.

02 · HOW IT WORKS

How does generative AI work?

Generative AI models learn patterns from large collections of examples. When you give a prompt, they predict and produce a likely response.

Remember: A confident-sounding answer can still be wrong.

03 · SCHOOL

How can AI help students?

It can offer another explanation, create practice questions, help brainstorm, or give feedback on a draft when a teacher allows it.

Remember: Use it to strengthen your understanding, not skip the learning.

04 · HONESTY

AI and academic integrity

Follow your teacher's directions. Be clear about when and how you used AI, and never submit generated work as your own if that isn't allowed.

Remember: Your ideas and effort should remain at the center.

05 · SAFETY

Privacy and personal information

Don't enter passwords, private records, personal contact details, or sensitive school information into an AI tool.

Remember: If you wouldn't post it publicly, don't put it into an AI prompt.

06 · CRITICAL THINKING

How do I check an AI answer?

Compare important claims with reliable sources, look for evidence, check dates, and ask whether the answer leaves out another point of view.

Remember: AI can help you investigate. It should not be your only source.

Our mission

Don't ban the tool.
Teach the skill.

AI for Schools supports a balanced approach: give students the knowledge to use AI thoughtfully while protecting learning, privacy, and academic honesty.

🎓

Keep learning first

AI should explain, coach, and support practice—not replace student effort or teacher instruction.

🧭

Set clear boundaries

Teachers should decide when AI is appropriate, and students should know how to disclose its use.

🔒

Protect students

Schools should review privacy, age suitability, accessibility, and safety before approving tools.

A practical school approach

Start small. Learn. Improve.

1. Pilot

Let volunteer teachers test approved tools for selected activities.

2. Teach

Show students how to fact-check, protect privacy, cite AI assistance, and recognize limitations.

3. Evaluate

Gather feedback from students, families, and teachers. Look at learning—not just usage.

4. Adjust

Keep what works, improve what doesn't, and update the rules as technology changes.

AI is already changing the world.

Students deserve the chance to understand it before they have to navigate it on their own.
